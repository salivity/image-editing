How to Fix Barrel Distortion in Image Editing Software

Barrel distortion is a common optical aberration caused by wide-angle lenses, making straight lines near the edges of a frame appear curved outward like the sides of a barrel. This guide outlines how to manually correct barrel distortion across most professional photo editing applications—such as Adobe Photoshop, Lightroom, Affinity Photo, and GIMP—by using grid overlays and manual transformation tools rather than relying on automatic lens profiles.

Step 1: Open the Image and Enable Alignment Grids

Begin by loading your image into your editing software. Before making adjustments, turn on the document grid or drag vertical and horizontal guides onto the canvas from your rulers. Place these guides near the outer thirds of the frame where straight architectural elements, horizons, or vertical pillars should normally align. These lines serve as your visual reference to ensure accurate correction.

Step 2: Open the Manual Lens Correction Tool

Locate the manual geometry or distortion panel within your software:

  • Photoshop: Go to Filter > Lens Correction, then select the Custom tab.
  • Lightroom / Camera Raw: Navigate to the Geometry or Lens Corrections panel and click the Manual tab.
  • GIMP: Select Filters > Distorts > Lens Distortion.
  • Affinity Photo: Go to Filters > Distort > Lens Correction.

Step 3: Adjust the Distortion Slider

Look for the slider labeled Distort, Remove Distortion, or Main.

  • Because barrel distortion pushes elements outward, you need to apply a compensating "pincushion" effect.
  • Move the slider slightly in the direction that pulls the outer edges inward (in Photoshop and Lightroom, this typically means dragging the slider to the right toward positive values; in other software, it may require a negative value).
  • Observe your guide lines. Adjust the slider until the curved lines along the edge of the image straighten and match the reference grid.

Step 4: Correct Perspective and Scale

Correcting barrel distortion frequently alters the scale of the photo and can expose empty, transparent pixels around the edges of the canvas.

  • Use the Scale slider within the correction tool to enlarge the image until the empty areas are pushed outside the visible frame.
  • If the image was shot at an angle, use the Vertical and Horizontal perspective sliders to balance any residual keystone effects.

Step 5: Fine-Tune with Free Transform or Warp (Optional)

If complex or asymmetrical distortion remains, confirm the lens correction changes and duplicate the layer. Use the Warp or Mesh Transform tool to manually grab control points near uneven edges and nudge them into alignment with your guides.

Step 6: Crop the Final Image

Once the lines are straight and the perspective is balanced, select the Crop Tool. Crop out any remaining transparent borders or distorted corners to complete the correction.
