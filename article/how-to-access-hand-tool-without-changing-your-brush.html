How to Access Hand Tool Without Changing Your Brush

Navigating a high-resolution canvas while maintaining a steady painting or retouching workflow requires quick, seamless panning. Instead of manually selecting the Hand Tool from the toolbar and interrupting your stroke, most modern image editing applications feature a built-in modifier shortcut. This guide explains how to temporarily activate the Hand Tool to move across your document and immediately return to your active brush without losing your settings.

The Universal Shortcut: Hold the Spacebar

The primary method to pan your canvas without switching tools is using the Spacebar:

  1. While using the Brush Tool (or any other active tool), press and hold down the Spacebar.
  2. The cursor will instantly change into the Hand Tool icon.
  3. Click and drag your mouse or stylus to pan across the canvas.
  4. Release the Spacebar. The tool will immediately revert to your previously selected brush, retaining all active brush dynamics, opacity, and size settings.

This shortcut is an industry standard and functions identically across major graphics software, including Adobe Photoshop, Affinity Photo, Clip Studio Paint, Krita, Corel Painter, and GIMP.

Spring-Loaded Tool Shortcuts

In applications like Adobe Photoshop, you can also use spring-loaded keys:

  • Press and hold the H key, click and drag to pan, then release the H key to snap directly back to the Brush Tool.
  • Additionally, holding H and clicking once allows you to use Photoshop's "Bird’s Eye View," zooming out to let you reposition your viewport before snapping back into your original zoom level.

Temporary Zoom Shortcuts

If you need to adjust your view scale alongside panning without dropping your brush, combine the Spacebar with your operating system’s modifier keys:

  • Zoom In: Hold Spacebar + Ctrl (Windows) or Spacebar + Command (macOS), then click or drag right to zoom in.
  • Zoom Out: Hold Spacebar + Alt (Windows) or Spacebar + Option (macOS), then click or drag left to zoom out.
  • Releasing the keys will instantly restore your brush cursor.

Configuring Graphics Tablets

If you work primarily with a drawing tablet or pen display, you can replicate this functionality entirely on your stylus:

  1. Open your tablet driver settings (e.g., Wacom Tablet Properties, Huion Tablet, or XP-Pen App).
  2. Locate the button configuration for your stylus.
  3. Assign one of the side buttons to keystroke Spacebar or map it directly to Pan/Scroll.
  4. Pressing and holding the stylus button while hovering or dragging will now pan the screen, and releasing it will immediately return you to drawing.
