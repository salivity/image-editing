Auto Perspective Correction Using Geometry Panel

The Geometry panel in modern photo editing software, such as Adobe Lightroom and Camera Raw, provides powerful automated tools designed to eliminate keystoning, tilted horizons, and lens-induced perspective distortion. This guide details how to locate the Geometry panel, apply one-click Upright modes to automatically straighten lines, and resolve common framing issues caused by perspective adjustments.

Locating the Geometry Panel

Open your raw or raster image in your editing application. In Adobe Lightroom Classic, switch to the Develop module and scroll down the right-hand panel until you locate the Geometry tab (in Lightroom CC and Camera Raw, look for the Geometry icon resembling a grid or overlapping geometric shapes). Expanding this tab reveals the Upright toolset and manual transform sliders.

Selecting the Right Upright Mode

The Upright feature uses intelligent algorithms to analyze horizontal and vertical lines in your image. To execute an automatic correction, select one of the following buttons based on your subject:

  • Auto: Applies a balanced correction that fixes both horizontal and vertical perspective issues while attempting to preserve as much of the original image area as possible. This is the best default choice for general photography.
  • Level: Detects the horizon or dominant horizontal lines and levels the image without adjusting vertical keystoning. Ideal for landscapes and seascapes.
  • Vertical: Focuses primarily on correcting vertical lines, forcing them into a parallel 90-degree alignment with the bottom frame. This is the optimal setting for cityscapes and architectural shots where tall buildings appear to lean backward.
  • Full: Combines maximum level and vertical corrections with distortion fixes. Use this when the image requires heavy, rigid architectural squaring, though it may introduce extreme cropping.

Managing Edges and Cropping

Applying automatic perspective correction warps the canvas, often leaving blank, unrendered wedges around the edges of the image.

To clean this up automatically, check the Constrain Crop box directly beneath the Upright modes. This automatically crops the image to the maximum possible rectangular area within the corrected image boundaries, eliminating transparent or white edges without requiring manual crop adjustments.

Fine-Tuning the Results

If the automatic setting overcorrects or changes the proportions of your subject, use the manual sliders located directly below the Upright modes to dial in the final look:

  • Scale: Zoom in or out to preserve key compositional elements near the edges.
  • Aspect: Adjust horizontally or vertically if the automatic correction stretched the subject unnaturally.
  • X/Y Offset: Shift the image along the horizontal or vertical axis to center the primary subject within the new perspective crop.
