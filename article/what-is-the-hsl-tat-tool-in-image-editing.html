What Is the HSL TAT Tool in Image Editing?

The HSL Targeted Adjustment Tool (TAT) is an interactive color-correction feature found in photo editing software such as Adobe Lightroom and Adobe Camera Raw. It allows editors to adjust Hue, Saturation, and Luminance directly on the image canvas rather than manually tweaking individual color sliders. By clicking and dragging over specific areas of a photo, the tool samples the exact pixel colors beneath the cursor and automatically adjusts the corresponding color channels, providing precise, intuitive, and efficient color grading.

What the HSL TAT Tool Does

The acronym HSL stands for Hue (the color shade), Saturation (the intensity of the color), and Luminance (the brightness of the color). In standard editing workflows, users modify these properties using eight dedicated color sliders: Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Aqua, Blue, Purple, and Magenta.

The Targeted Adjustment Tool simplifies this workflow by replacing the sliders with a direct, on-image control. Represented by a small crosshair or target icon in the HSL/Color panel, the TAT allows you to select which property you want to affect—Hue, Saturation, or Luminance—and apply changes directly to the pixels on the screen.

How the TAT Samples Specific Colors

Digital images rarely contain pure, single-spectrum colors. For example, human skin tones are typically a blend of orange, red, and yellow, while foliage often contains both green and yellow values. Manually estimating which sliders to move and by what proportion can lead to unnatural color shifts.

The TAT solves this through real-time pixel sampling:

  1. Pixel Readout: When you place the TAT cursor over a specific area of your photograph, the tool reads the underlying RGB values of those pixels.
  2. Channel Mapping: The software instantly calculates which HSL color bands contribute to the sampled area and determines their exact proportions.
  3. Interactive Adjustment: When you click and drag upward or downward (or left and right, depending on the software settings), the tool dynamically adjusts the values. If an area consists of 70% orange and 30% red, dragging the tool will automatically adjust the Orange and Red sliders simultaneously in that exact proportion.

Benefits in the Editing Workflow

Using the HSL TAT eliminates guesswork, particularly when working with complex gradients such as sunsets, skies, and skin tones. It prevents color banding and unnatural artifacts by ensuring that all overlapping color spectrums present in a target area move in harmony, resulting in smoother and more realistic color adjustments.
