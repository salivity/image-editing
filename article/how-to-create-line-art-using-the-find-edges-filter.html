How to Create Line Art Using the Find Edges Filter

The Find Edges filter is a powerful tool in raster image editing software like Adobe Photoshop and GIMP that identifies sharp transitions in color and brightness to generate an outline of your subject. This article explains how to prepare your source image, apply the filter, and refine the resulting strokes to create a clean, usable line art base for digital illustration, coloring, or graphic design.

Step 1: Duplicate and Prepare the Base Image

Open your source image in your editor of choice. Before making any changes, duplicate the background layer using Ctrl + J (Windows) or Cmd + J (macOS). Working on a duplicate preserves your original photo and allows you to reference it later.

Convert this duplicate layer to grayscale by desaturating it (Ctrl/Cmd + Shift + U). Removing color ensures the edge-detection algorithm focuses purely on tonal contrast rather than color shifts, resulting in a cleaner outline.

Step 2: Reduce Noise with a Slight Blur

The Find Edges filter detects pixel-level contrast, which means image noise, film grain, or fine textures will be converted into messy lines. To prevent this, apply a subtle blur to your desaturated layer:

  1. Go to Filter > Blur > Gaussian Blur (or Surface Blur).
  2. Set the radius between 0.5 and 2.0 pixels depending on the image resolution.
  3. The goal is to smooth out micro-textures while keeping defining silhouettes and major contours crisp.

Step 3: Apply the Find Edges Filter

Select your prepared layer and navigate to Filter > Stylize > Find Edges.

The software will automatically analyze pixel boundaries and draw dark outlines against a light background. If your software renders white lines on a dark background instead, simply invert the layer colors using Ctrl + I or Cmd + I.

Step 4: Refine Contrast with Levels or Curves

The initial output of Find Edges is usually faint, with distracting mid-tones and residual artifacts. To transform it into high-contrast line art:

  1. Open the Levels adjustment tool using Ctrl + L or Cmd + L.
  2. Drag the Black Input Slider to the right to darken and solidify the lines.
  3. Drag the White Input Slider to the left to wash out unwanted gray shading into pure white.
  4. Adjust the middle gray slider until the line weight looks balanced and clean.

Step 5: Set the Blend Mode and Clean Up

To integrate the new line art over other layers:

  1. Change the line art layer's blend mode from Normal to Multiply. This makes all white areas completely transparent, leaving only the dark strokes visible.
  2. Use a hard round eraser or a white brush to remove any lingering artifacts, stray marks, or unwanted background details.
  3. If necessary, lock the layer transparency and paint over the lines with a solid color to customize your line work.
