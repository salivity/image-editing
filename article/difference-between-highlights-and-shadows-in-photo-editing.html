Difference Between Highlights and Shadows in Photo Editing

Understanding the difference between highlights and shadows is fundamental to mastering photo editing, as these two controls govern the brightest and darkest tonal ranges of an image. Highlights represent the areas receiving the most illumination without being pure white, while shadows account for the darker portions of the frame that contain information just above pure black. By manipulating these opposing ends of the tonal spectrum, editors can recover lost details, balance dynamic range, and establish the overall mood and contrast of a photograph.

What Are Highlights?

Highlights are the brightest areas within an image that still retain visible detail, texture, and color. Examples include the soft glow of sunlight on a subject's skin, reflections on water, or the texture of bright clouds in the sky.

In photo editing software, adjusting the highlights slider targets this specific bright luminance range without significantly affecting the midtones or the extreme pure whites.

  • Decreasing Highlights: Pulls detail back into overexposed or overly bright areas, restoring texture in skies or pale surfaces.
  • Increasing Highlights: Adds vibrancy, energy, and perceived brightness to the illuminated parts of the scene.

What Are Shadows?

Shadows represent the darker tonal regions of an image where light is blocked or minimal, yet detail is still present. Examples include the shaded side of a building, foliage beneath a tree canopy, or dark clothing.

The shadows slider in editing software isolates these low-luminance zones without altering the true blacks or the midtones.

  • Increasing Shadows: Lifts the darkness to reveal hidden textures and subjects that were underexposed, balancing high-contrast scenes.
  • Decreasing Shadows: Deepens dark areas, creating a richer, moodier, and more dramatic look by increasing overall contrast.

Key Differences Between Highlights and Shadows

Feature Highlights Shadows
Histogram Position Far right side (just below pure white) Far left side (just above pure black)
Visual Function Defines light sources, reflections, and sheen Defines depth, dimension, and structure
Correction Purpose Prevents blown-out, detail-less bright areas Prevents crushed, muddy dark areas
Primary Goal Controlled downward to recover bright details Controlled upward to recover dark details

Highlights vs. Whites and Shadows vs. Blacks

A common point of confusion is how highlights and shadows differ from whites and blacks:

  • Highlights vs. Whites: The highlights slider adjusts bright tones while preserving detail. The whites slider sets the absolute white point—the threshold where pixels turn into pure, clipped white with zero detail.
  • Shadows vs. Blacks: The shadows slider reveals or conceals details within the darker portions. The blacks slider establishes the true black point—the threshold where pixels become solid, featureless black.

How to Balance Both in an Edit

Balancing highlights and shadows is key to managing dynamic range. In high-contrast environments—such as a landscape shot at midday—the camera sensor often struggles to capture both the bright sky and the dark ground simultaneously.

By pulling down the highlights to recover cloud definitions and pushing up the shadows to reveal foliage and ground details, you compress the dynamic range into an even, natural exposure that closely mimics what the human eye perceives.
