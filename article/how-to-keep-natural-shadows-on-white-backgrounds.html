How to Keep Natural Shadows on White Backgrounds

Extracting an object onto a pure white background while retaining its original, natural shadow is essential for creating realistic, professional product photos and composites. Rather than drawing an artificial shadow from scratch, this technique uses layer duplication, blending modes, and tonal adjustments to separate the subject, isolate the cast shadow, and seamlessly blend it onto a pure white canvas.

Step 1: Duplicate the Source Layer

Open your image and duplicate the original background layer twice. You should now have three identical layers in your stack. Rename the top layer to "Subject," the middle layer to "Shadow," and keep the bottom layer as your original reference.

Step 2: Cut Out the Subject

Select the "Subject" layer. Use a precise selection tool—such as the Pen Tool, Quick Selection, or the Object Selection Tool—to outline only the object, deliberately excluding any part of the shadow. Once selected, click the Layer Mask icon to hide the original background.

Step 3: Insert a Pure White Background

Create a new Solid Color adjustment layer, set the color to pure white (#FFFFFF), and position it directly beneath your "Shadow" layer. At this stage, hide the "Shadow" layer to verify that your "Subject" cutout is clean against the white backdrop.

Step 4: Isolate the Shadow with Blending Modes

Turn the "Shadow" layer back on and change its blending mode from "Normal" to "Multiply." The Multiply mode eliminates white pixels while keeping darker pixels visible, allowing the cast shadow to show through onto the white solid layer beneath it.

Step 5: Clean the Background Tones

Because the original background is rarely pure white, the Multiply layer may leave a gray tint across the canvas. To fix this:

  1. Add a Levels or Curves adjustment layer directly above the "Shadow" layer and clip it to that layer (Alt/Option + Click between the layers).
  2. Move the white point slider inward until the surrounding gray areas turn completely pure white and disappear, leaving only the shadow visible.
  3. If the shadow has an unwanted color cast from ambient lighting, add a Black & White or Hue/Saturation adjustment layer clipped to the shadow layer to desaturate it.

Step 6: Refine the Shadow Edges

Apply a layer mask to the "Shadow" layer. Use a soft black brush with low opacity to paint away any remaining artifacts, dust, or unwanted dark areas far from the object. Finally, lower the layer opacity slightly if the shadow appears too harsh, ensuring it blends naturally with the isolated subject above it.
