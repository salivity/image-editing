How to Contour a Face Using Dodge and Burn

Contouring a face using dodge and burn is a non-destructive retouching technique that enhances facial bone structure by manually manipulating highlights and shadows. Rather than using makeup, photo editors selectively lighten (dodge) areas where light naturally hits the face and darken (burn) areas where shadows fall, creating three-dimensional depth and dimension while preserving the subject's natural skin texture. This guide outlines the professional workflow for setting up your document, applying proper brush techniques, placing contours accurately according to facial anatomy, and finalizing the effect for a polished, natural result.

1. Document Setup for Non-Destructive Editing

The most controlled method for dodge and burn contouring is using adjustment layers with inverted layer masks:

  • Create two separate Curves adjustment layers.
  • Name the first layer "Dodge" and pull the center of the RGB curve upward to brighten the midtones.
  • Name the second layer "Burn" and pull the center of the RGB curve downward to darken the midtones.
  • Invert both layer masks to pure black (Ctrl+I or Cmd+I) to completely hide the adjustments.
  • Group both layers into a folder titled "Global D&B" or "Contour."

2. Brush Configuration

Precise control over the intensity of your brush strokes is essential to avoid harsh, muddy lines:

  • Select a standard Soft Round Brush with 0% hardness.
  • Set the brush color to pure white (#FFFFFF) to reveal the adjustments on your masks.
  • Set the brush Opacity to 100% and Flow to 1% to 3%. Using a very low flow allows you to gradually build up light and shadow with overlapping strokes, similar to traditional painting.

3. Applying the Dodge (Highlights)

Select the mask of the "Dodge" layer. Apply strokes to the raised planes of the face that catch natural ambient light:

  • Cheekbones: Sweep along the top ridge of the cheekbones, moving outward toward the temples.
  • Nose: Paint a narrow, straight line down the center ridge of the bridge, tapering off before the tip, with a subtle dab on the very tip itself.
  • Forehead: Lightly brush an inverted triangle or soft circle in the lower-middle portion of the forehead, just above the eyebrows.
  • Chin: Add a small highlight to the center dome of the chin.
  • Cupid’s Bow: Gently brush along the upper curve of the top lip to define the mouth.
  • Brow Bone: Add a subtle stroke directly beneath the outer arch of each eyebrow.

4. Applying the Burn (Shadows)

Switch to the mask of the "Burn" layer to carve out depth and define the facial perimeter:

  • Cheek Hollows: Paint directly beneath the cheekbones, angling downward from the top of the ear toward the corner of the mouth, tapering before reaching the lips.
  • Jawline: Trace along the bottom edge of the mandible and directly beneath the jaw to create separation between the face and the neck.
  • Temples and Hairline: Sweep along the upper perimeter of the forehead and into the temples to narrow the forehead and frame the face.
  • Sides of the Nose: Run delicate, thin lines down the lateral slopes of the nose bridge to slim and straighten its appearance.

5. Refining and Balancing

Once your placement is complete, assess the contour to ensure realism:

  • Zoom out to view the entire image at a distance; contouring is meant to read correctly from a wide perspective rather than up close.
  • Toggle the visibility of the layer group on and off to evaluate the transition from the original lighting.
  • If the effect looks exaggerated, greasy, or artificial, lower the Opacity of the entire layer group (typically settling between 40% and 70%) until the sculpted planes integrate smoothly with the original skin tones.
