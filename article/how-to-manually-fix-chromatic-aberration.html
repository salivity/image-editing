How to Manually Fix Chromatic Aberration

Chromatic aberration, often seen as unsightly purple, green, red, or cyan fringing along high-contrast edges, occurs when a camera lens fails to focus all color wavelengths to the same convergence point. While automated lens profiles can eliminate mild distortion, severe or unique fringing requires manual adjustment. This guide explains how to use the manual controls inside the Lens Correction filter to target and eliminate color fringing precisely without degrading image sharpness.

Step 1: Open the Lens Correction Filter

Open your image in your photo editing software, such as Adobe Photoshop. Convert your image layer into a Smart Object by right-clicking the layer and selecting "Convert to Smart Object" to ensure your edits remain non-destructive. Navigate to the top menu and select Filter > Lens Correction.

Step 2: Switch to the Custom Tab

By default, the Lens Correction workspace opens to the Auto Correction panel. Click on the Custom tab located on the right side of the screen. This panel provides granular control over geometric distortion, chromatic aberration, and vignetting.

Step 3: Zoom In on High-Contrast Edges

To accurately see the fringing, set your zoom level to at least 100% or 200%. Pan across your image to locate areas with harsh transitions between dark and bright elements, such as tree branches against an overexposed sky or backlit architectural silhouettes.

Step 4: Identify the Fringe Colors

Examine the color of the halo appearing on the edges:

  • Red/Cyan Fringing: One side of an edge will display a reddish tint, while the opposing side shows a cyan tint.
  • Blue/Yellow Fringing: One side will display a deep blue tint, while the opposite side shows a warm yellow edge.
  • Green/Magenta Fringing: Often prominent in out-of-focus areas or strong backlight, displaying magenta on one side and green on the other.

Step 5: Adjust the Aberration Sliders

Under the Chromatic Aberration section in the Custom panel, move the sliders corresponding to the colors you identified:

  • Fix Red/Cyan Fringe: Drag the slider left or right until the red and cyan edges align and disappear into the subject.
  • Fix Blue/Yellow Fringe: Move this slider to balance and neutralize yellow and blue separation.
  • Fix Green/Magenta Fringe: If available in your software version, slide this control to eliminate axial fringing along contrast boundaries.

Make minor adjustments in increments of 1 to 3 units. Moving the sliders too far will introduce new fringing of the opposite color cast.

Step 6: Toggle the Preview and Apply

Click the Preview checkbox on and off to evaluate your adjustments against the original image. Ensure the edge detail remains crisp and that no secondary color artifacts have formed along adjacent edges. Once satisfied, click OK to apply the filter.
