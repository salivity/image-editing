How to Naturally Enhance Eye Catchlights in Editing

Catchlights—the bright reflections of a light source in a subject's eyes—add life, depth, and emotion to a portrait. While flat or dim lighting can leave eyes looking dull, over-editing catchlights can result in an unnatural, alien-like appearance. This guide provides a straightforward, step-by-step approach to naturally enhancing existing catchlights using standard photo editing software like Adobe Photoshop, Lightroom, or Capture One, focusing on targeted adjustments, tonal balance, and edge feathering.

Step 1: Work with Existing Reflections

Never invent catchlights where no reflection exists, as mismatched angles will confuse the viewer's brain and look artificial. Zoom in to 100% or 200% and identify the natural catchlight created by the key light, reflector, or ambient environment. Your goal is to amplify what is already there rather than draw new shapes from scratch.

Step 2: Create a Targeted Adjustment Layer

In your editing software, isolate the catchlight using a precise local adjustment tool:

  • Photoshop: Add a Curves adjustment layer. Invert the layer mask to black (Ctrl+I or Cmd+I), then use a small, soft white brush set to roughly 20–30% opacity to paint over the catchlight reflection.
  • Lightroom/Camera Raw: Use the Brush or Radial Gradient tool directly over the reflection.

Step 3: Lift the Highlights Without Blowing Out Detail

On your adjustment layer:

  • Raise the midtones and highlights slightly by curving upward in the upper-right quadrant of the Curves graph.
  • If using sliders, moderately increase Exposure (+0.30 to +0.60 EV) and boost Whites.
  • Avoid pushing the whites to pure 255 clipping across the entire reflection. A natural catchlight features a bright center that gently transitions into softer highlights near the edges.

Step 4: Refine the Color Temperature

Catchlights reflect the actual light source used in the shot. If your key light was a warm window or a neutral softbox, the catchlight should reflect that exact tone. Neutralize any strange color casts by slightly adjusting the Temperature and Tint within your mask so the reflection appears clean, crisp, and harmonious with the rest of the skin tones.

Step 5: Contrast the Iris for Greater Impact

Often, making a catchlight pop is not about making the reflection brighter, but about creating contrast with the surrounding area:

  • Create a second local adjustment layer to darken the pupil and the limbal ring (the dark outer border of the iris) slightly.
  • Gently lift the exposure of the iris opposite the catchlight to mimic natural subsurface scattering of light through the eye.
  • This contrast creates natural optical dimension without requiring you to over-brighten the catchlight itself.

Step 6: Soften Edges and Dial Back Opacity

Harsh, digital-looking catchlights usually suffer from edges that are too sharp.

  • Feather your mask slightly to ensure the reflection blends into the cornea.
  • Zoom out to fit the entire portrait on your screen.
  • Lower the opacity of your adjustment layer to 0%, then slowly raise it until the eyes appear bright and engaged without immediately drawing attention to the edit. If the edit is obvious at full view, reduce the layer opacity by another 10% to 15%.
