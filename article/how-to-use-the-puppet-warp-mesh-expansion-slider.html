How to Use the Puppet Warp Mesh Expansion Slider

The Puppet Warp tool allows precise control over image distortion by overlaying an adjustable triangular mesh onto a selected subject. The Mesh Expansion slider plays a critical role in this process by expanding or contracting the outer perimeter of this mesh beyond the original selection boundaries. Mastering this setting ensures that soft edges, semi-transparent details, and hair are fully captured during warping, while also preventing unwanted background pixels from being caught in the transformation.

Locating the Mesh Expansion Setting

To access the slider, you must first isolate your subject onto its own layer and activate the Puppet Warp tool:

  1. Select your target layer.
  2. Navigate to Edit > Puppet Warp.
  3. In the top Options bar, ensure the Show Mesh checkbox is selected so the polygonal grid becomes visible.
  4. Locate the Expansion input box and slider located directly in the Options bar next to the mesh density controls.

How the Mesh Expansion Slider Works

The Expansion control is measured in pixels and dictates how far the boundary of the mesh extends past the transparent edges of your cutout:

  • Increasing Expansion (Positive Values): Sliding the value upward expands the mesh outward. This is essential when warping subjects with soft, feathered edges, frizzy hair, or motion blur. If the mesh is too tight, these outer pixels remain static while the interior moves, resulting in harsh, unnatural seams or clipped borders.
  • Decreasing Expansion (Negative Values): Sliding the value downward or keeping it low shrinks the mesh closer to the opaque pixels of the subject. This is useful when working with hard-edged, crisp subjects, or when an imperfect cutout risks dragging remnants of the background into the warp.

Step-by-Step Usage Guide

  1. Evaluate the Edges: Zoom in on your subject to check the edge quality. Hard-edged objects (like machinery or graphics) require minimal expansion, while soft-edged subjects (like portraits or fabric) require more.
  2. Adjust the Slider: Use the slider or type a pixel value to visually expand the mesh until it completely envelops all fringed or semi-transparent edge pixels. Avoid setting it excessively high, as an overly large mesh can inadvertently grab empty canvas area or cause overlapping distortion issues.
  3. Set Your Pins: Place your control pins on key joints or structural anchor points within the mesh.
  4. Transform and Monitor: Drag the pins to reposition your subject. Watch the outer boundaries carefully to verify that all edges move cohesively without clipping or leaving disconnected fragments behind.
  5. Commit the Changes: Press Enter or click the checkmark in the Options bar to apply the transformation.
