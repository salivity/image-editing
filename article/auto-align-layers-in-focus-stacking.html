Auto-Align Layers in Focus Stacking

The auto-align layers feature in image editing software is used to precisely register and match multiple photos before merging them into a single, sharp image through focus stacking. Focus stacking requires shooting a sequence of photos at varying focal distances, but minor movements between frames—such as camera shake, wind, or lens breathing—can cause misalignments. Auto-aligning layers corrects these discrepancies automatically, ensuring that the sharpest areas of each exposure blend seamlessly without producing ghosting artifacts or blur.

When capturing a focus stack, maintaining exact pixel-for-pixel alignment across every shot is nearly impossible. Even when using a sturdy tripod, pressing the shutter, slight environmental vibrations, or mechanical movement inside the camera can shift the frame by a few pixels. For photographers working handheld, these shifts are even more pronounced.

Beyond physical movement, "lens breathing" is the primary reason auto-alignment is mandatory. Lens breathing occurs when adjusting the focus ring alters the actual focal length and magnification of the lens slightly. As focus shifts from the foreground to the background, the subject will naturally appear to grow or shrink across sequential frames.

The auto-align layers tool solves these issues by analyzing the pixel data across all selected layers. It automatically identifies shared reference points, high-contrast edges, and geometric features throughout the images. Using these reference points, the software applies precise micro-adjustments to each individual layer:

  • Translation: Shifts layers horizontally and vertically to correct lateral movement.
  • Scale: Enlarges or reduces layers to neutralize the size changes caused by lens breathing.
  • Rotation: Corrects slight rotational tilting caused by camera movement.
  • Perspective/Distortion: Adjusts for minor angle changes to ensure flat, uniform registration.

Skipping the auto-alignment phase typically ruins the final focus stack. If layers are blended while misaligned, the composite will display severe edge halos, double images (ghosting), and smeared textures where the software fails to match the out-of-focus and in-focus zones. By running auto-align layers first, editors establish a geometrically consistent base that enables the subsequent auto-blend process to accurately mask and combine only the sharpest pixels into a deep, edge-to-edge depth of field.
