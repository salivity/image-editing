What Are LUTs and How to Apply Them in Image Editing

This article provides an essential guide to Look-Up Tables (LUTs) in modern image editing. You will learn what LUTs are, how they mathematically transform colors, the differences between technical and creative variations, and a step-by-step method for integrating them into your editing workflow to achieve consistent, professional color grading.

What Is a LUT?

A LUT, or Look-Up Table, is a file containing a set of mathematical instructions used to alter the color values of an image. In simple terms, it takes the original color input (RGB values) from your photograph and remaps it to a predefined output color.

While filters or simple presets often adjust multiple individual sliders (like exposure, contrast, and saturation), a LUT directly converts specific hues and tones across the color spectrum. This makes LUTs an industry standard for precise, repeatable color transformation in both photography and videography.

Types of LUTs

In image editing, LUTs generally fall into two categories:

  • Technical (Conversion) LUTs: Designed to normalize images. For example, if you shoot in a flat color profile (like Log), a technical LUT converts the flat, low-contrast image into a standard color space, such as sRGB, establishing accurate contrast and saturation.
  • Creative LUTs: Used for stylistic purposes. These LUTs impart a specific mood or aesthetic, such as a vintage film look, warm cinematic tones, or cool, muted color palettes.

How to Apply LUTs in an Image Editing Workflow

To achieve the best results, a LUT should rarely be the very first or only step. Follow this structured workflow to apply them cleanly:

1. Perform Base Corrections

Before applying a LUT, balance your raw image. Correct the exposure, white balance, contrast, and tone curves. Because LUTs are calibrated to standard, well-exposed inputs, applying one to an underexposed or incorrectly balanced image will distort the final colors unpredictably.

2. Apply the LUT on a Non-Destructive Layer

Import the LUT using your software's dedicated tool. In Adobe Photoshop, add a Color Lookup adjustment layer and load your .CUBE or .3DL file. In Lightroom or Camera Raw, LUTs are typically loaded as Profiles. Working on an adjustment layer ensures your original image remains untouched.

3. Adjust the Intensity

LUTs are often aggressive at 100% strength. Lower the layer opacity (usually between 30% and 70%) to blend the effect naturally with your original colors. This avoids harsh clipping in the highlights and shadows while maintaining the desired aesthetic.

4. Fine-Tune Secondary Colors

Once the LUT is applied, refine the image. You may need to tweak specific skin tones, reduce contrast in the shadows, or adjust the highlights to ensure the final result looks cohesive and natural.
