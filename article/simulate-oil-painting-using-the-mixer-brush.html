Simulate Oil Painting Using the Mixer Brush

Digital artists can transform photos and digital sketches into traditional-looking artwork by leveraging the mixer brush tool to simulate the texture, blending, and viscosity of real oil paint. This guide provides a straightforward breakdown of how to prepare your canvas, configure critical brush settings, and apply directional brushwork to replicate authentic oil painting aesthetics non-destructively.

Preparing the Canvas

To preserve the original image and paint flexibly, duplicate your source image onto a background layer. Create a new, blank layer directly above it where the actual painting will occur. Select the Mixer Brush tool from your toolbar and ensure that the "Sample All Layers" option is checked in the top options bar. This configuration allows your brush to sample colors directly from the base image below while keeping all wet paint strokes isolated on the separate layer.

Configuring the Mixer Brush Parameters

The Mixer Brush relies on four key parameters to emulate the physical behavior of wet oils on canvas:

  • Wet: Determines how much paint is picked up from the canvas. Set this between 20% and 50% for standard blending. Higher values create a watercolor-like glaze, while lower values behave like thick, dry paint.
  • Load: Dictates how much paint fills the brush reservoir. A moderate setting around 30% to 50% mimics a freshly dipped brush that gradually deposits paint as you stroke.
  • Mix: Controls the balance between the paint loaded on the brush and the paint already on the canvas. A mix of 40% to 60% provides a natural blend without completely overriding existing colors.
  • Flow: Regulates how quickly paint leaves the brush tip. Keep this between 80% and 100% to maintain steady, full-bodied strokes.

Additionally, toggle on the "Clean the brush after each stroke" icon and toggle off the "Load the brush after each stroke" icon. This setup prevents your brush from carrying sampled colors infinitely, allowing you to blend existing colors on the canvas dynamically.

Choosing the Right Brush Preset

Smooth, standard round brushes cannot replicate the tactile feel of oil paint. Open your brush presets and select a bristle brush that displays individual hair textures, such as a flat bristle or a fan brush. Ensure brush dynamics—specifically shape dynamics and transfer controlled by pen pressure—are enabled so that varied pressure alters the stroke's width and opacity naturally.

Applying Directional Strokes

Rather than painting randomly across the image, follow the cross-contours and natural forms of the underlying subject. Use shorter, curved strokes across rounded surfaces like faces or drapery to define volume. For backgrounds, use broader, cross-hatching motions to soften hard edges and create atmospheric depth. Vary your stroke length and pressure constantly to prevent the digital canvas from looking uniform or mechanically generated.

Adding Surface Texture and Depth

True oil paintings feature subtle impasto where paint builds up in thick ridges. Once your main brushwork is complete, you can emphasize this physical presence. Duplicate your painted layer, apply a gentle High Pass filter, and set the blending mode to Soft Light or Overlay. This step sharpens the brush bristle marks and mimics the way light catches the edges of dried oil paint. Pairing this with a subtle canvas texture overlay set to a low opacity completes the traditional illusion.
