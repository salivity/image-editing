Creating Stone Effects with Bevel and Emboss

Applying a realistic stone texture to text or shapes relies heavily on configuring the Bevel and Emboss layer style paired with surface texture mapping in modern image editing software. This guide explains how to prepare your base layer, dial in the proper chisel structure and lighting settings, apply a high-contrast pattern within the Texture sub-menu, and fine-tune depth controls to produce an authentic, chiseled rock appearance.

Step 1: Prepare the Base Element

Begin with your text or vector shape on a transparent layer. Apply a neutral, mid-tone base color—such as a muted slate gray (#5A5A5A) or sandstone beige—so the upcoming highlight and shadow calculations have sufficient tonal range to display surface depth accurately.

Step 2: Configure the Primary Bevel Structure

Double-click the layer to open the Layer Styles dialog and select Bevel and Emboss. Adjust the core structure parameters to simulate cut rock:

  • Style: Set to Inner Bevel.
  • Technique: Change the default "Smooth" setting to Chisel Hard. This replaces smooth transitions with rigid, facet-like edges that replicate carved stone.
  • Depth: Increase between 150% and 300% to create pronounced, steep ridges.
  • Size: Adjust according to the dimensions of your object. A value between 5 px and 15 px generally works best for medium-sized text.
  • Soften: Set to 0 px (or a maximum of 1 px) to maintain sharp, fractured boundaries along the edges.

Step 3: Calibrate Shading and Directional Light

Realistic stone requires high-contrast directional lighting to cast appropriate crevice shadows.

  • Angle and Altitude: Use a standard light source angle, such as 120 degrees with an altitude of 30 degrees. Ensure "Use Global Light" is checked if matching other scene elements.
  • Highlight Mode: Keep set to Screen, using a pure white or very pale warm tint, adjusted to an opacity of 60% to 75%.
  • Shadow Mode: Keep set to Multiply, using pure black or a deep cool tone, adjusted to an opacity of 75% to 85% to emphasize heavy fissures.

Step 4: Apply the Texture Setting

Click directly on the Texture option nested immediately beneath Bevel and Emboss to reveal the surface patterning controls:

  • Pattern: Open the pattern picker and select an organic, high-contrast texture such as concrete, granite, rust, or heavy noise.
  • Scale: Adjust the slider to match the scale of the element. Lower values (around 25% to 50%) generate a fine, granular sandstone appearance, while higher values create large, craggy slabs.
  • Depth: Set this parameter to control the physical relief of the surface. A positive depth (+20% to +60%) pushes the texture outward, whereas negative values make the texture appear pitted and eroded. Keep the depth subtle enough that it does not completely obscure the base shape's silhouette.
  • Invert: Toggle this option to evaluate whether raised peaks or carved grooves look more natural against your chosen pattern.
  • Link with Layer: Leave this box checked so the texture remains locked in place if the element is repositioned on the canvas.

Step 5: Final Refinement

To finalize the stone finish, add a subtle Inner Shadow set to Multiply at low opacity (20% to 30%) to add depth along the outer perimeters, or apply a low-opacity Gradient Overlay to introduce natural weathering and uneven mineral staining across the face of the stone.
