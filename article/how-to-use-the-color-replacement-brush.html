How to Use the Color Replacement Brush

The Color Replacement Brush is a powerful photo editing tool designed to change the color of specific areas in an image while preserving the underlying texture, shadows, and highlights. This guide explains how to configure and apply the Color Replacement Brush for quick, realistic hue shifts. By adjusting settings such as blend modes, sampling methods, and tolerance, you can precisely isolate target colors and recolor objects in just a few straightforward steps.

Step 1: Duplicate Your Layer and Select the Tool

Always begin by duplicating your background layer to ensure non-destructive editing. In your photo editor (such as Adobe Photoshop), navigate to the Tools panel, click and hold the standard Brush Tool, and select the Color Replacement Tool from the fly-out menu.

Step 2: Set Your Target Color

Choose the color you want to apply by setting your Foreground Color in the color picker. The tool will apply this exact hue while retaining the original brightness and saturation profiles of the underlying pixels.

Step 3: Configure the Tool Options

Before painting, adjust the settings in the top options bar to match your image requirements:

  • Mode: Set the blend mode to Color (which changes both hue and saturation) or Hue (which changes only the tint while leaving the original saturation intact).
  • Sampling:
    • Continuous: Constantly samples colors as you drag the cursor, ideal for areas with multiple shades.
    • Once: Samples the color only where you first click, ideal for uniform, solid-colored objects.
    • Background Swatch: Replaces only the area matching your current background color.
  • Limits: Choose Contiguous to color only connected pixels matching the sampled area, or Discontiguous to color matching pixels even if they are separated by different colors.
  • Tolerance: Set the sensitivity slider. A lower percentage (20–30%) restricts the brush to shades closely matching your initial click, preventing color bleed. A higher percentage (50–70%) covers a broader range of color variations.

Step 4: Paint Over the Target Area

Place the crosshair inside the cursor directly over the color you want to change, click, and drag. Keep the center crosshair confined to the target object; the brush will only replace pixels within the outer circle that match the color under the crosshair.

Handling Edge Cases

Because the Color Replacement Brush relies on existing luminosity and saturation to blend properly, it is ineffective on pure white, deep black, or completely neutral gray areas. For best results, use this tool on objects that already have a defined hue and moderate saturation levels. If the edges look unnatural or bleed into the surrounding background, undo the stroke, lower the tolerance, and enable Anti-aliasing for smoother boundaries.
