How to Remove Green Tint from Photos

An unwanted green tint, often caused by fluorescent lighting, incorrect white balance, or foliage reflections, can ruin an otherwise great photograph. This guide provides clear, actionable methods to neutralize green color casts using standard image editing tools like Adobe Photoshop, Lightroom, or free alternatives like GIMP. By adjusting tint sliders, using target eyedroppers, fine-tuning color curves, or editing specific color channels, you can quickly restore natural, balanced tones to your images.

1. Adjust the Tint Slider (Global White Balance)

The fastest way to eliminate an overall green cast is by balancing the temperature and tint settings.

  • Open your image in the editor's raw processor or color adjustment panel (such as Lightroom's Basic panel or Photoshop's Camera Raw filter).
  • Locate the Tint slider, which controls the Green-Magenta color axis.
  • Drag the slider toward Magenta (to the right) until the green cast disappears and skin tones or neutral areas look natural.
  • Make small adjustments, as moving the slider too far can introduce an unnatural purple hue.

2. Use the White Balance Eyedropper

If your photo contains an element that should be pure white, neutral gray, or black, the eyedropper tool can automatically calculate and correct the color balance.

  • Select the White Balance Tool (eyedropper icon).
  • Click on a neutral area within the image, such as a gray card, a white shirt, or concrete.
  • The software will automatically shift the red, green, and blue values to make that targeted spot neutral, neutralizing the green tint across the entire photo.

3. Fine-Tune with the Curves Tool

Curves provide precise control over shadows, midtones, and highlights independently.

  • Add a Curves adjustment layer.
  • Switch the channel dropdown from RGB to Green.
  • Click the center of the diagonal line (representing the midtones) and pull it slightly downward. Pulling the green curve down subtracts green and adds magenta.
  • If the green tint is only visible in dark areas or bright highlights, adjust the lower-left (shadows) or upper-right (highlights) portion of the curve rather than the center.

4. Target Localized Casts with HSL / Color Panels

If the green cast only affects specific areas, such as light bouncing from grass onto a subject's skin, a global adjustment may desaturate the rest of the scene.

  • Open the HSL (Hue, Saturation, Luminance) or Color Mixer panel.
  • Select the Saturation tab, locate Green and Yellow, and lower their sliders slightly to reduce the intensity of the cast.
  • Alternatively, use a brush or mask to paint over the affected area, then increase the magenta tint exclusively within that selection.
