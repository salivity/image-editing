HDR Merging Guide for Real Estate Photography

High Dynamic Range (HDR) merging in real estate image editing is the process of combining multiple bracketed exposures into a single, well-balanced photograph that accurately represents both bright interior highlights and crisp outdoor window views. This guide outlines the exact, step-by-step workflow required to merge real estate brackets correctly, eliminate artifacts, manage mixed lighting temperatures, and produce crisp, natural-looking property listings.

1. Import and Organize Bracketed Sets

Organize the raw files into their respective exposure brackets. Most real estate shoots use 3 to 5 exposures spaced 1 to 2 stops (EV) apart, capturing the darkest shadows, the ambient room lighting, and the brightest window views. Ensure files are grouped chronologically to prevent mixing shots from different rooms.

2. Apply Pre-Merge Corrections

Before merging, apply universal corrections across the entire bracketed sequence:

  • Lens Profile Corrections: Enable profile corrections to eliminate distortion and optical vignetting.
  • Chromatic Aberration: Check the box to remove chromatic aberration to avoid color fringing around high-contrast areas like window frames.

3. Merge the Exposures

Use your preferred software (such as Adobe Lightroom, Photoshop, or Photomatix) to merge the bracketed raw files:

  • Auto-Align: Always check this option to correct for subtle camera movements, even when using a sturdy tripod.
  • Auto-Settings: Use this as a baseline, but be prepared to adjust manually to avoid an overly flat or unnatural look.
  • Deghosting: Set deghosting to low or medium if there is movement caused by ceiling fans, swaying curtains, or plants outside the windows.

4. Perform the Window Pull (Exposure Blending)

Automated HDR merges frequently blow out the view through windows or leave them looking washed out. To fix this:

  1. Open the merged file alongside the darkest ambient or flash exposure in Photoshop as layers.
  2. Place the dark exposure layer (where the window view is properly exposed) underneath or above the merged image.
  3. Use a layer mask or the "Luminosity Mask" feature to brush in the clear, vibrant exterior view while keeping the natural frame of the window intact.

5. Correct Mixed Color Temperatures

Interiors often feature warm incandescent light mixed with cool daylight entering from the windows. To resolve color casts:

  • Set the global white balance using a neutral surface in the room (such as a white ceiling or neutral wall).
  • Use local adjustment brushes or selective color tools to desaturate persistent orange casts from lamps or blue spills on floors near windows.

6. Straighten Verticals and Geometry

Real estate images must have straight, vertical lines:

  • Use the Upright or Transform tool (such as the Guided Upright tool in Lightroom/Photoshop) to align walls, door frames, and corners.
  • Ensure the camera perspective matches a natural standing height.

7. Final Polish and Export

Make final, subtle global adjustments to finish the image:

  • Highlights and Shadows: Lower highlights slightly to retain texture in ceilings and brighten shadows to open up dark corners.
  • Contrast and Clarity: Add a small amount of contrast and clarity to give the image punch, avoiding excessive clarity which makes HDR images look muddy.
  • Sharpening: Apply moderate sharpening with a mask to protect smooth surfaces like walls from digital noise.
  • Export: Export as high-resolution sRGB JPEGs optimized for the MLS or real estate listing platforms.
