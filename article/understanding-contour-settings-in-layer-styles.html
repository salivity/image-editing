Understanding Contour Settings in Layer Styles

The contour setting in image editing software is an advanced control used within layer styles to alter the shape, distribution, and falloff of an applied effect. This article explains how contour mappings manipulate light and shadow transitions across effects like Bevel & Emboss, Drop Shadow, and Glows, enabling digital designers to create realistic surfaces like metal, glass, and plastic without manual painting.

The Core Function of Contour

At its foundation, a contour is an input/output curve mapping that controls how an effect fades from its starting point to its outer boundary. By default, most layer styles transition in a straight, linear path: a drop shadow steadily fades from dark to transparent, and a bevel slopes evenly from the edge to the peak.

The contour setting interrupts this linear progression by remapping values across a graph. It allows values to spike, drop, plateau, or reverse along the effect's transition zone, drastically altering how the effect behaves across space.

Contour in Lighting and 3D Effects

Contour is most prominently used in bevel and emboss settings, where it appears in two distinct forms:

  • Surface Contour: This controls the physical shape and curvature of the edge. Changing the contour profile transforms a basic flat bevel into a rounded bead, a sharp cone, a rippled frame, or a carved chisel.
  • Gloss Contour: This alters how light reflects off the simulated 3D surface. A standard linear gloss contour creates a matte finish, while complex curves with sharp peaks and valleys simulate the high-contrast reflections characteristic of chrome, polished metal, liquid, and glossy plastic.

Contour in Shadows and Glows

When applied to drop shadows, inner shadows, or glows, contour determines opacity falloff:

  • Linear contours produce standard, soft-edged shadows.
  • Curved or stepped contours create hard-edged rings, halos, or neon-like multi-line effects.
  • By modifying the curve, an artist can create deep, sudden shadow drops or diffuse, ambient light spreads that mimic complex ambient occlusion.

Contour Profiles: Presets vs. Custom Curves

Image editing software generally includes standard preset curves alongside an editor for custom adjustments:

  • Linear: A direct, 45-degree angle representing an even, standard transition.
  • Gaussian / Half-Round: A curved falloff producing smooth, natural, organic edges.
  • Cone / Peak: Creates sharp, steep highlights or ridges.
  • Ring / Double Ring: Produces metallic reflections, light refraction, or multiple outline borders.
  • Custom Curves: Users can click on the contour graph to add anchor points, adjusting the curve manually with corner points or bezier handles to achieve exact material finishes.

Mastering contour settings allows digital artists to move beyond flat, procedural layer effects to generate intricate, tactile, and highly customized textures directly within non-destructive layer styles.
